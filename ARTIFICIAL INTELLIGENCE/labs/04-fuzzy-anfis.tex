\begin{labquestion}{2}{Build the fuzzy controller}
Inputs: temperature and relative humidity. Output: ventilation demand, 0--100\,\%. Give each variable three triangular membership functions (the outer two are shoulders), write the expert rules, and implement Mamdani inference: \texttt{min} for AND, \texttt{max} to combine rules, centroid to defuzzify. Plot the memberships.
\end{labquestion}
\begin{labanswer}
Follow the dictionaries \texttt{t} and \texttt{h}, then the three rule strengths, then \texttt{output}. The numerator sums \texttt{V\_range} times \texttt{output}, and the denominator sums \texttt{output}. Their ratio at the warm-up reading is 50, not the firing strength 0.8.

Six rules map temperature and humidity to low, medium or high ventilation, and the centroid of the combined output set is the crisp percentage.

\begin{labquestion}{5}{Fit two model classes}
Train linear regression as the baseline. Implement a first-order Sugeno ANFIS with Gaussian memberships: the rule outputs are solved by least squares, the membership centres and widths by L-BFGS-B. Train candidates with two and three memberships per input and select the size by validation RMSE only. Save \texttt{metrics.csv}.
\end{labquestion}

\end{lstlisting}
\end{refoutput}

\textbf{What it means.} Both ANFIS candidates beat the linear baseline on validation RMSE, and three memberships per input is marginally better than two, so it is selected. The choice is made without looking at the test columns. The class maps onto the five ANFIS layers: Gaussian memberships (layer 1), rule strengths and their normalisation (layers 2--3) in \texttt{\_weights}, linear rule outputs (layer 4) in \texttt{\_design}, and the weighted sum (layer 5) in \texttt{predict}. L-BFGS-B differs slightly between SciPy versions, so your third decimal may differ from this reference; the ordering of the models should not.
\end{labanswer}

\begin{expectedfindings}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Medium fires at $0.40$ and high at $0.11$, and the answer is $55.5\,\%$. Adding weight on the high side moves the balance point above 50.
  \item At $(25,80)$ nothing fires: the reading is mild and humid, and ``humid'' was the only rule covering it. The output set is empty, so the centroid is $0/0$ and the function returns \texttt{nan} with a warning. 99 of the 1,886 grid points have the same problem, whereas the full rule base has none. Coverage must be tested, not assumed.
  \item 16 rules and a validation RMSE of about 0.445, against about 0.448 for 9 rules. The gain is small for nearly twice the rules, so staying with three sets is easy to defend. There is no single right answer here; the reasoning is the point.
\end{enumerate}
\end{expectedfindings}
