\begin{labquestion}{3}{Check admissibility empirically}
An admissible heuristic never overestimates the true remaining cost. Run Dijkstra once on the reversed graph to get the exact cost from every airport \emph{to} the goal, then compare $h$ with that exact cost for ten airports spread across the range. Save \texttt{heuristic\_check.csv}.
\end{labquestion}
\begin{labanswer}
\lstinputlisting[style=utppython,firstline=52,lastline=90]{all_experiments/lab02_informed_search_worked.py}
\begin{refoutput}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=utpterminal]
Admissibility violations: 0 of 30
\end{lstlisting}
\end{refoutput}

\textbf{What it means.} Zero violations in thirty checks: the slack column is never negative, and it runs from 0 up to about 26,000~km. A straight-line distance can never exceed the length of any actual route between the same two points, so Haversine is admissible and consistent by the triangle inequality. The table is an empirical check; the geometry is the guarantee.
\end{labanswer}

\begin{labquestion}{5}{Stress the guarantee}
Repeat the queries with the inflated heuristic $1.5\,h$ (weighted A$^\star$). Explain when the returned cost changes.
\end{labquestion}
\begin{labanswer}
\lstinputlisting[style=utppython,firstline=107,lastline=117]{all_experiments/lab02_informed_search_worked.py}

\textbf{What it means.} Multiplying $h$ by 1.5 makes it inadmissible: it can now overestimate the remaining distance, so a route that \emph{looks} closer to the goal is expanded first and returned. The cost changes when the optimal route detours through airports that are geographically further from the goal than a rival hop (Q1 and Q3). When the optimal route already heads straight for the goal (Q2) nothing changes. Expansions fall to a handful, and the price is a route up to 7\,\% longer.
\end{labanswer}

\begin{expectedfindings}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Exactly the UCS results: same cost and same expansions (1,851, 3,002 and 3,042). A$^\star$ with $h=0$ \emph{is} UCS.
  \item Cost stays 11,461~km up to $w=1$, while expansions fall from 1,851 to 1,210, 399 and 69. At $w=1.1$ the cost rises to 11,483~km with 4 expansions, at $w=1.2$ to 11,584~km, at $w=1.5$ and $w=2$ to 12,274~km, and at $w=5$ to 12,500~km with 2 expansions, the same route as GBFS. A larger admissible guess saves work for free; beyond $w=1$ the saving starts to cost quality.
  \item Miles keep the guarantee, because $h$ only got smaller (same route, 1,001 expansions). Metres break it, because $h$ is now 1,000 times too large and swamps $g$ (the 12,500~km GBFS route, 2 expansions).
\end{enumerate}
\end{expectedfindings}
