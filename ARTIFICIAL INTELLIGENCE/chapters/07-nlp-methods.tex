\subsection{N-grams}

\term{Bigrams} add adjacent token pairs such as ``never again''. They increase features from 4,671 to 11,850 in the lab, making the matrix sparser. Use validation performance to decide whether they help.

\section{Multinomial Naive Bayes}

Naive Bayes chooses the class $c$ that maximises the posterior probability of the document $d$:
\[
\hat c=\argmax_c\; P(c)\prod_{w\in d}P(w\mid c)^{n_w},
\]
Here $n_w$ counts term $w$, $P(c)$ is the training class share, and $P(w\mid c)$ its term probability. The ``naive'' assumption treats terms as independent given the class; despite its limitations for language, it is useful (\figref{fig:naive-bayes}).

\subsection{Smoothing}

An unseen class--term pair has probability zero, eliminating that class from the product. \term{Laplace smoothing} adds pseudo-count $\alpha$ to each term:
\[
P(w\mid c)=\frac{\text{count}(w,c)+\alpha}{\sum_{w'}\text{count}(w',c)+\alpha\,|V|}.
\]
Small $\alpha$ trusts observed counts; large $\alpha$ moves probabilities towards uniform. Log sums prevent numerical underflow. Lab~7 selects $\alpha=0.3$ from $\{0.3,0.5,1,2\}$ on validation data; n-grams matter more than smoothing here.

\begin{intuition}
Each term contributes evidence for each class based on training counts. Smoothing prevents an unseen term from eliminating a class.
\end{intuition}

\section{Metrics for imbalanced classes}

Tweets are 63\,\% negative, 21\,\% neutral and 16\,\% positive. Always predicting negative achieves 63\,\% accuracy, so report per-class metrics too:

\begin{mltable}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries\leavevmode\color{MLNavy}}p{0.16\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{MLPaleBlue!92}
Metric & Definition & Answers \\
\midrule
Precision & Of the tweets predicted as class $c$, the fraction that are $c$ & When the model says positive, how often is it right? \\
Recall & Of the tweets that are $c$, the fraction predicted as $c$ & How many of the positive tweets did it find? \\
F1 & Harmonic mean of precision and recall & One number per class that punishes neglecting either \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{mltable}

\term{Macro F1} weights class F1 scores equally. The majority baseline has recall~1 for negative and~0 otherwise, giving macro F1 $0.26$; Naive Bayes reaches $0.70$.

\begin{pitfall}
Compute the majority baseline first: accuracy rises from 63\,\% to 77\,\%, while macro F1 rises from $0.26$ to $0.70$. Together they show what the model adds.
\end{pitfall}

\section{A leakage-safe protocol}

\term{Leakage} occurs when test information influences training or model selection. Lab~7 controls it at each stage (\figref{fig:protocol}).

\begin{aifig}[The evaluation protocol of Lab~7]{Deduplicate before splitting, fit vocabulary and model on training data, select settings on validation data, and evaluate once on test data.}{fig:protocol}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small,
  s/.style={aibox,minimum width=2.6cm,minimum height=1.0cm,font=\scriptsize\color{MLNavy},align=center}]
\node[s] (dedup) at (0,0) {\textbf{1. Deduplicate}\\exact and normalised\\copies removed};
\node[s,right=0.45cm of dedup] (split) {\textbf{2. Split 70/15/15}\\stratified, seed 42\\saved to split.csv};
\node[s,right=0.45cm of split,fill=MLPaleOrange,draw=MLOrange] (fit) {\textbf{3. Fit on train}\\vocabulary and\\Naive Bayes};
\node[s,right=0.45cm of fit,fill=MLPaleOrange,draw=MLOrange] (tune) {\textbf{4. Tune on validation}\\n-grams and $\alpha$\\by macro F1};
\node[s,right=0.45cm of tune,fill=MLPaleTeal,draw=MLGreen] (test) {\textbf{5. Test once}\\metrics, confusion\\matrix, errors};
\foreach \a/\b in {dedup/split, split/fit, fit/tune, tune/test}{\draw[aiarrow] (\a)--(\b);}
\node[ailabel,anchor=north,color=MLOrange] at (7.05,-0.75) {sees training and validation rows only};
\node[ailabel,anchor=north,color=MLGreen] at (11.9,-0.75) {sees test rows: once};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{aifig}

\begin{description}
  \item[Duplicates before the split.] There are 213 exact duplicates and 355 duplicates after URL and mention replacement. Keep one copy of each normalised text before splitting to prevent shared answers.
  \item[Stratified, seeded, saved.] Preserve class proportions in a seeded 70/15/15 split and save the assignment for audit.
  \item[Vocabulary from training text only.] Fit \texttt{CountVectorizer} on training tweets, then transform validation and test tweets. Test-only words remain unknown.
  \item[Selection on validation, one look at test.] Compare eight settings on validation data, refit the winner, then evaluate once on test data.
\end{description}

\begin{keyidea}
Deduplicate, split, fit, tune, test. Respecting these data boundaries keeps the final score separate from model development.
\end{keyidea}

\section{Reading the evidence}

The lab produces four kinds of evidence, each answering a question the headline score cannot.

\begin{description}
  \item[The confusion matrix.] The $3\times3$ table shows neutral tweets often predicted as negative: questions about delays share complaint vocabulary.
  \item[Per-class scores.] Negative F1 $0.86$, positive $0.67$, neutral $0.56$. Neutral is the hardest class: it has the fewest distinctive words and sits between the other two.
  \item[Per-group scores.] Airline macro F1 ranges from $0.66$ to $0.78$, with 63--594 test tweets per airline. Different sample sizes, class mixes and topics make this a diagnostic, not an airline ranking.
  \item[An error sample.] Tag the four most confident mistakes per true class (twelve tweets). The reproducible sample reveals missed negation, low-sentiment questions, short texts and a repeatedly retweeted joke with inconsistent labels. The last is a data-quality issue, not just a model error.
\end{description}

\begin{pitfall}
Every sampled error has posterior above $0.999$. Confidence is not correctness: use these uncalibrated Naive Bayes scores for ranking, not as reliable probabilities of being right.
\end{pitfall}

\section{What the protocol does not fix}

Bag-of-words Naive Bayes struggles with negation, sarcasm and word order. The deliverable asks what representation to try next: weighted counts, embeddings or contextual models. Keep the deduplicated split, validation-based selection and single final test evaluation unchanged.
