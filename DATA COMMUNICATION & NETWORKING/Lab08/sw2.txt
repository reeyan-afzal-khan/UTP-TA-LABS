! ============================================================
! Lab 8 --- SW2 VLAN configuration
!
! The VLANs are created AGAIN here. VLAN membership does not travel
! across a trunk: the trunk carries tagged frames, but the receiving
! switch must already know the VLAN or it discards them.
! ============================================================

system-view
sysname SW2

! Same VLAN IDs as SW1. A VLAN that exists on only one switch is the
! second most common failure in this lab (after native-VLAN mismatch).
vlan batch 10 20 30 999

! ---------- Access ports ----------
interface GigabitEthernet 0/0/1
 port link-type access
 port default vlan 10                 ! PC3, 192.168.10.2/24
quit

interface GigabitEthernet 0/0/2
 port link-type access
 port default vlan 20                 ! PC4, 192.168.20.2/24
quit

! ---------- Trunk to SW1 ----------
! Every setting here must MIRROR SW1. The trunk is a negotiation
! between two ends; a value that differs is not overridden, it just
! produces a fault whose symptom appears somewhere else.
interface Eth-Trunk 1
 port link-type trunk
 ! Native VLAN 999, identical to SW1. If SW1 said 999 and this said
 ! the default 1, VLAN 999 frames from SW1 would arrive untagged and
 ! be filed into VLAN 1 here --- a silent cross-VLAN leak.
 port trunk pvid vlan 999
 port trunk allow-pass vlan 10 20 30
quit

quit
save

! ---------- Verify ----------
! display vlan
!   VLANs 10, 20, 30, 999 all present. Compare against SW1.
!
! display port vlan
!   Confirm the trunk's PVID reads 999 on BOTH switches, and that the
!   allow-pass list really contains 10, 20 and 30 --- on some VRP
!   versions a second allow-pass REPLACES the list instead of adding
!   to it, silently dropping the VLANs you configured first.
!
! ---------- The experiment from vlan_matrix.md ----------
! Temporarily set  port trunk pvid vlan 20  here while SW1 stays at 10,
! then ping between VLAN 10 and VLAN 20. It will SUCCEED --- and that
! success is the bug: SW1 sends VLAN 10 untagged, SW2 receives an
! untagged frame and assigns it to VLAN 20. Two isolated VLANs joined,
! with no error logged on either switch.
!
! Set both ends back to 999 and confirm isolation returns.
