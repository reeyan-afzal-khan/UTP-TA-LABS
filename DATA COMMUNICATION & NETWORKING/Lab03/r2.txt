! ============================================================
! Lab 3 --- R2 static routing (transit router)
!
! R2 carries traffic between R1 and R3 but owns NO LAN of its own.
! That makes it the clearest illustration of the rule: a router
! forwards only what it has a route for, in BOTH directions.
! ============================================================

system-view
sysname R2

interface GigabitEthernet 0/0/0
 ip address 10.0.12.2 30
 undo shutdown
quit

interface GigabitEthernet 0/0/1
 ip address 10.0.23.2 30
 undo shutdown
quit

! ---------- STOP. Verify before adding routes. ----------
! display ip routing-table
!   Two Direct entries, nothing else. R2 can reach both its
!   neighbours and neither LAN.

! ---------- Routes for BOTH LANs ----------
! A transit router needs a route toward every destination it must
! carry --- one for the forward path, one for the return path.
! Configure only the first and PC1 -> PC3 still fails: the request
! arrives at LAN 3 and the reply dies here at R2.
ip route-static 192.168.3.0 24 10.0.23.3      ! toward LAN 3, via R3
ip route-static 192.168.1.0 24 10.0.12.1      ! back toward LAN 1, via R1

quit
save

! ---------- Verify ----------
! display ip routing-table protocol static
!   Two static entries, both preference 60.
!

! the R1 <-> R3 backup link instead. R2 is simply bypassed --- its
! routing table does not change at all.
