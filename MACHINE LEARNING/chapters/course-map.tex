\section*{How to use this book}

Each chapter opens with an everyday picture, works a small example you can check with a pen, and only then gives the formal version. Each lab sheet comes straight after the chapters it practises.

\begin{utpsteps}
  \item \textbf{Read with a pen.} Redo the worked examples before reading the answer. Every number in them is printed by a program in \texttt{all\_experiments/}, so you can check yourself twice.
  \item \textbf{Answer the check at the end of the chapter.} The answers follow immediately, so cover them first.
  \item \textbf{Do the lab in the notebook.} Each sheet gives setup steps, a warm-up on paper, questions numbered to match the notebook, reference outputs, and what those outputs mean.
  \item \textbf{Finish with \emph{Your turn}.} These tasks go beyond the worked answers. Record your own numbers first, then read the expected findings.
  \item \textbf{Hand in.} Work through the verification and submission checklists at the end of the sheet.
\end{utpsteps}

\begin{sourcebasis}
The order of the core methods and several analogies across the book (the three-step recipe, the bowl with one lowest point, the hill in fog, the dimmer switch, confident and wrong, the rubber bands, the landmarks, the shadow on a wall, and the question ``does your log hold the answers?'') are adapted from a video lecture, \href{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=42IVf4m-5fk}{an hour on machine learning}. The tutor's study log, the datasets, the code and all numbers in this book are our own, and every one can be reproduced by running the programs in \texttt{all\_experiments/}.
\end{sourcebasis}
