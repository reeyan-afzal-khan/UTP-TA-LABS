\begin{labquestion}{3}{Score $k$ from 2 to 8}
K-means needs $k$ in advance, so run it for every $k$ from 2 to 8 and record two internal scores. \emph{Inertia} is the total squared distance from each customer to its cluster center, and it always falls as $k$ grows. The \emph{silhouette} compares how close each customer is to its own cluster against the nearest other cluster: higher means better separated, and it can go up or down.
\end{labquestion}

\labpart{Part B --- K-means versus DBSCAN}

\begin{labquestion}{4}{Compare K-means with DBSCAN}
Two clustering philosophies. K-means assigns every customer to one of $k$ round groups around a center. DBSCAN grows groups from dense regions and leaves customers in sparse areas unassigned, with the label $-1$ for noise. It needs no $k$, but it needs a radius \texttt{eps} and a minimum neighbor count.
\end{labquestion}
\begin{labanswer}
\lstinputlisting[style=utppython,firstline=62,lastline=73]{all_experiments/lab04_clustering_pca_worked.py}
\begin{refoutput}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=utpterminal]
K-means silhouette: 0.417
DBSCAN clusters: 1  noise points: 14
\end{lstlisting}
\end{refoutput}

\textbf{What it means.} With this radius DBSCAN sees one continuous cloud and a handful of stragglers. That is not a failure. It says there are no density gaps between the K-means groups.
\end{labanswer}

\begin{labquestion}{8}{Choose a PCA budget on validation}
PCA finds the directions in which the 64 pixel values vary most, and keeps only the top few. The question is how many. Because a classifier will use the compressed images, the fair judge is that classifier's accuracy on a validation split carved from the training file. Three budgets are tried: keep 90\%, 95\% or 99\% of the variance.
\end{labquestion}
\begin{labanswer}

\end{refoutput}

\textbf{What it means.} The 95\% budget uses less than half of the numbers and scores within a rounding error of all 64. The PCA sits inside the pipeline, so it learns from the fitting rows only.

\begin{expectedfindings}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Six of eight reach 168.2. Seeds 2 and 4 stop at 180.1 and 194.4. That is why Question~4 runs 30 starts and keeps the best.
  \item Raw against scaled agree at an adjusted Rand index of 0.602, so scaling changed the segments substantially. After the income rescale the index against income alone is 1.000: age and spending no longer count at all. The clustering follows the units unless you decide otherwise.
  \item At $k=6$, the young group of 54 splits into a lower-income group of 24 who spend a lot (age 25, income 26, score 77) and a middle-income group of 38 (age 27, income 58, score 48), while the rest shift slightly. A team that targets by budget may well want that split, which is an argument for six that the silhouette alone cannot make.
  \item Validation accuracy is 0.608 with 2 components, 0.868 with 5 and 0.934 with 10, against 0.954 with 29. Two components keep 28.4\% of the variance. They are good for drawing a picture, not for classifying digits.
\end{enumerate}
\end{expectedfindings}

\begin{troubleshoot}
\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{KeyError: 'optdigits.tra'}] The archive was re-zipped with different names, or it was unzipped and the zip removed. Restore the original \texttt{.zip}.
  \item[A memory-leak warning from K-means on Windows] Set \texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS=1} before importing scikit-learn, as the Setup cell does. If you ran cells out of order, restart the kernel.
  \item[The cluster numbers differ from the sheet] Numbers are arbitrary names. Match clusters by their profile values and sizes.
  \item[DBSCAN finds nothing but noise] Check that you are passing the scaled \texttt{X}, not the raw table. On raw units \texttt{eps=0.75} is far too small.
\end{description}
\end{troubleshoot}
