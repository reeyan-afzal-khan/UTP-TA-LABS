  \item \textbf{Count support vectors.} Refit the winning SVM from Question~11 on the training loans and read \texttt{n\_support\_}. How many training loans does the boundary depend on, and how many of them are defaults?
  \item \textbf{Whose model is it?} Refit Question~4's logistic regression without \texttt{int\_rate}, then without \texttt{int\_rate} and \texttt{term\_months}, then on \texttt{int\_rate} alone. How much of the model's skill is the lender's own pricing?
\end{enumerate}
\end{yourturn}

\begin{expectedfindings}
\textbf{Warm-up.} Always saying ``repaid'' scores $4{,}041/5{,}000=80.8\%$, and the models below differ far more in how they rank risky loans than in accuracy. In raw units the amounts differ by 5,000 and the scores by 20, so the loan amount decides the distance alone; after scaling the gaps are $5{,}000/8{,}487=0.59$ and $20/30.3=0.66$, and the credit score counts slightly more.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Cross-validated ROC AUC falls from 0.719 to 0.571, and validation ROC AUC from 0.723 to 0.586. The loan amount has a standard deviation of \$8,487, against 30.3 for the FICO score, 23.7 for revolving utilization and 4.3 for the interest rate, so in raw units the neighbors are simply loans of a similar size, and the size of a loan says little about its risk.
  \item Log-RMSE is 0.2836 with 1 tree, 0.2135 with 10, 0.2067 with 50, 0.2055 with 150 and 0.2053 with 400. It stops improving much after about 50 trees: more trees steady the average, but they cannot go below the error the trees share. Always guessing the mean scores 0.6553.
  \item 11,478 of the 15,000 training loans, about three quarters, and 2,205 of them are among the 2,878 training defaults. On noisy data most loans sit near the boundary or on its wrong side, so the SVM keeps them all: that is why the file is 1.4~MB, and why SVMs grow slow as the rows grow.
  \item The score falls from 0.731 to 0.708 without the interest rate, and to 0.669 without the rate and the term; the rate alone scores 0.707. One column, set by the lender from its own grade, carries almost all of the skill. A model trained on it mostly reproduces the lender's assessment, which is useful, but it is no independent second opinion.
\end{enumerate}
\end{expectedfindings}

\begin{troubleshoot}

  \item[The SVM cells take a minute] Question~11 fits eleven settings three times each on 10,000 loans, and an SVM's training time grows faster than its rows. That is expected on a laptop CPU.
  \item[Accuracy looks fine for every model] It is: always saying ``repaid'' is 80.8\% accurate. Judge the models by ROC AUC and average precision, as the sheet does.
  \item[\texttt{ValueError: Input X contains NaN}] A tree, a plot or a model was given loans with gaps. Keep the imputer in every pipeline, and use the filled copies \texttt{Xt\_train} and \texttt{Xt\_valid} for the trees.
  \item[A loky or joblib message when the kernel stops] \texttt{n\_jobs=-1} sometimes prints a harmless resource-tracker message on Windows when a pool closes. If the cell's results printed, the run is fine.
\end{description}
